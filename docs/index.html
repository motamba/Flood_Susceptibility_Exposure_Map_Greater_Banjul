    About

    This map shows where flooding is most likely in Greater Banjul (Banjul, Kanifing Municipal Council and Kombo North), based on terrain and land cover. It also shows which assets lie in the most susceptible areas.

    Method

    1. Terrain: a bare-earth elevation model (FABDEM, 30 m) is reprojected and its hollows breached so water can be routed. Streams, open water and mapped drains form the drainage network.
    2. Five factors, each scaled 0–1: height above the nearest drainage (weight 0.30), elevation (0.20), depth of hollows where water ponds (0.20), wetness index (0.15) and land cover (0.15).
    3. Index and classes: the weighted sum is split into Low, Medium and High with natural breaks (Jenks).
    4. Sensitivity: 11 alternative weightings. The High zone keeps 80–94% overlap under moderate changes. Elevation is the most influential factor.
    5. Exposure: OpenStreetMap buildings, roads, schools and health facilities are counted per class.
    6. Plausibility: 5 of 8 places reported as flooded in the news lie in High, which covers 36% of the land.

    Limitations

    • 30 m elevation cannot see drains, culverts or walls. Blocked drains, a common cause of flooding here, are not in the model.
    • There is no observed flood map to validate against. The check against news reports uses only 8 places.
    • The weights are expert judgement. Exposure counts change with them (23–43% of buildings in High).
    • OpenStreetMap is incomplete, so all counts are minimum values.
    • Rainfall cells are about 5.5 km wide and smooth out local downpours.
